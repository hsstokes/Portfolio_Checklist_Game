Workshop Overview

Key Considerations for Your Portfolio

Objective: This session is about ensuring your portfolio meets the assessment criteria and tells your story clearly and creatively.

You will work through a checklist game to review key areas of your portfolio, then document your progress with screenshots and reflection notes.

How it works: You'll complete two cycles. In each cycle, you'll choose one area from the checklist to review, make improvements to your portfolio, document your progress, and upload to Padlet.

You Will Learn To:

  • Review portfolio against criteria
  • Identify areas for improvement
  • Reference sources correctly
  • Document your creative process
  • Reflect on your work

Recap: How to Reference an Artist

📚 Harvard Referencing Style for Images

Have you properly credited your sources? Use this format for citing artists:

Format:

Artist's Last Name, First Initial. (Year). Title of the work [Image].

Example:

Turner, L. (2018). Abstract concepts in digital art [Image].
Important: Proper referencing shows your research skills and respects the work of other artists and designers.

Portfolio Checklist — Choose ONE Area

0 / 12 Complete
Project Title: Does your introduction have a clear, engaging title that reflects your project?

A good project title should:

  • Be creative and memorable
  • Reflect your theme or concept
  • Set the tone for your portfolio
Project Context: Have you clearly explained the background and context of your project?

Context should include:

  • What inspired your project
  • The problem or theme you're exploring
  • Your personal connection to the subject
Visual Interest: Does your introduction include imagery that captures attention and helps visualise your theme?

Strong visuals should:

  • Create immediate impact
  • Relate to your theme
  • Be high quality images
Portfolio Roadmap: Does your introduction provide a clear roadmap of what follows?

A roadmap helps the viewer:

  • Understand the structure of your portfolio
  • Know what to expect
  • Navigate your work easily
🎉 Great job! You've completed the Introduction Page checklist! 🎉
Subheadings & Layout: Are subheadings clear, concise, and creative? Have you included graphic elements?

Good subheadings:

  • Define what aspect of research you're exploring
  • Help expand creative ideas
  • Include visual hierarchy
Research & Inspiration: Have you included quotes to support your knowledge?

Strong research pages show:

  • Diverse research sources
  • Your own responses to what you've found
  • Quotes that support your ideas
Design Development: Do your pages show a relationship between 2D and 3D ideas? Have you included research links to support your design development?

Design development should include:

  • Both 2D and 3D ideas
  • A mix of whole designs and detail elements
  • Research links supporting development
Refinement & Selection: Do you show a variety of similar but different design ideas from which to select, edit, and inform your final lineup?

Refinement pages should show:

  • A selecting and editing process
  • Various similar but different ideas
  • Ticks or marks to show decisions
Referencing: Have you properly credited your sources? (Harvard Referencing Guide)

Proper referencing includes:

  • Artist name and year
  • Title of work in italics
  • [Image] designation
Colour Palette: Have you selected a relevant palette and included research links to support your ideas?

A strong colour palette:

  • Connects to your research theme
  • Shows colour theory understanding
  • Links to inspiration sources
Typography: Is your font choice consistent, creative and appropriate for your theme?

Good typography:

  • Is consistent throughout
  • Reflects your project theme
  • Is readable and well-sized
Visual Elements: Do your illustrations, photographs, and diagrams enhance your message?

Strong visual elements:

  • Support and enhance your content
  • Are high quality
  • Create visual interest
