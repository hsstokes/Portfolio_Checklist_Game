Recap: how to reference an artist

📚 Harvard style for images

Artist's Last Name, First Initial. (Year). Title of the work [Image].

Example: Turner, L. (2018). Abstract concepts in digital art [Image].

Good referencing shows your research skills. It respects other artists' work.

🔤 Typography: "If your text is shouting, your design can't breathe."
  • Body text: 12–14pt, left- or right-aligned in blocks
  • Subtitles: use a different style, not just a bigger size
  • Create flow: text guides the eye – it doesn't block it
  • Suggested fonts: Orator ST, Andale Mono, Avenir Next Condensed, Helvetica Neue, Bodoni, Didot, Futura

Portfolio Checklist — choose ONE area0 / 12 complete
Project Title: Does your introduction have a clear, engaging title that reflects your project?

A good project title should:

  • Be creative and memorable
  • Reflect your theme or concept
  • Set the tone for your portfolio
Project Context: Have you clearly explained the background and context of your project?

Context should include:

  • What inspired your project
  • The problem or theme you're exploring
  • Your personal connection to the subject
Visual Interest: Does your introduction include imagery that captures attention and helps show your theme?

Strong visuals should:

  • Create immediate impact
  • Relate to your theme
  • Be high quality images
Portfolio Roadmap: Does your introduction give a clear roadmap of what follows?

A roadmap helps the viewer:

  • Understand the structure of your portfolio
  • Know what to expect
  • Navigate your work easily

Subheadings & Layout: Are subheadings clear, concise and creative? Have you included graphic elements?

Good subheadings:

  • Define what part of your research you're exploring
  • Help expand creative ideas
  • Include visual hierarchy

Need help? See Generative Fill Subheadings workshop

Research & Inspiration: Have you included quotes to support your knowledge?

Strong research pages show:

  • Different research sources
  • Your own responses to what you found
  • Quotes that support your ideas

Need help? See Enlarging Details & Artist Links workshop

Design Development: Do your pages show a relationship between 2D and 3D ideas? Have you included research links?

Design development should include:

  • Both 2D and 3D ideas
  • A mix of whole designs and detail elements
  • Research links supporting development

Need help with textures? See Monoprint workshop or Stencils & Brushes workshop

Refinement & Selection: Do you show similar but different design ideas, to select, edit and inform your final lineup?

Refinement pages should show:

  • A selecting and editing process
  • Various similar but different ideas
  • Ticks or marks to show decisions
Referencing: Have you properly credited your sources? (Harvard Referencing Guide)

Proper referencing includes:

  • Artist name and year
  • Title of work in italics
  • [Image] designation

Need help adding text? See Text on Path workshop

Colour Palette: Have you chosen a relevant palette with research links to support your ideas?

A strong colour palette:

  • Connects to your research theme
  • Shows colour theory understanding
  • Links to inspiration sources
Typography: Is your font choice consistent, creative and right for your theme? Is your text sized correctly?

Good typography:

  • Body text: 12–14pt maximum, left- or right-aligned in blocks
  • Subtitles: a different style (weight, font, colour) – not just bigger
  • Hierarchy: contrast through weight, spacing and colour
  • Consistency: ONE heading font + ONE body font
  • Flow: text guides the eye, it doesn't block it
  • Reflects your project theme

"If your text is shouting, your design can't breathe."

Suggested fonts: Orator ST, Andale Mono, Avenir Next Condensed, Helvetica Neue, Bodoni, Didot, Futura

Want a unique font? See Handwriting to Digital Font workshop

Visual Elements: Do your illustrations, photographs and diagrams support your message?

Strong visual elements:

  • Support and enhance your content
  • Are high quality
  • Create visual interest

Need more visual interest?

  • Pattern Brushes workshop – create visual links
  • Fun Foam Printmaking – add handmade textures
  • Moving Elements workshop – reposition and blend

Step 1 · Export pages from Photoshop (300 ppi)

Make sure the page is finished. Each portfolio page should be a separate PSD file.

Go to Image › Image Size (Cmd + Option + I Mac / Ctrl + Alt + I Windows).

Resolution must be 300 pixels/inch (ppi).

Cmd + Option + Shift + W Mac / Ctrl + Alt + Shift + W Windows.

Or: use File › Save As and choose JPEG.

  • Format: JPG
  • Quality: 100% (maximum)
  • Embed Color Profile: ticked (sRGB recommended)

Using Save As? Set Quality to 12 (maximum).

Start each name with a number:

  • 01_TitlePage.jpg
  • 02_Introduction.jpg
  • 03_Research_01.jpg
  • 04_Research_02.jpg
  • …and so on

The PDF Builder sorts your pages automatically!

Export each page as a separate JPEG. You should have 24–30 JPEG files.

⚠️ Important

Export all pages at 300 ppi for high-quality print. This is essential for your submission!

Step 2 · Use the PDF Builder

Fill in your name, student number, unit title and IPF group at the top.

  • Drag all your JPEGs onto the "Drag & Drop Multiple Pages Here" area
  • Or click a page slot to upload one at a time

Several files at once are sorted by file name.

  • Drag pages to swap positions
  • Click X to remove a page
  • Click Preview to see all pages

When you have 24–30 pages, click Download PDF.

Your file is named: Portfolio_YourName_StudentNumber_Date.pdf

💡 PDF Builder tips
  • Your progress saves automatically – you can close the browser and come back
  • Use the Checklist Guide button to review requirements
  • Aim for 24–30 pages
  • Pages should be A3 Landscape
  • All pages 300 ppi
